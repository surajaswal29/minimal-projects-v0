This season

What the garden is asking for

Spring

The soil warms a little each week. Work gently, sow little and often, and let the early weeds tell you the ground is ready.

Sow

  • Peas and broad beans
  • Radish, spinach, rocket
  • Sweet peas by the fence

Notice

  • Blackthorn blossom before its leaves
  • The first bumblebee queens
  • Birdsong starting before six

Rest

  • Don't rush tender plants out
  • Leave some dandelions for bees
  • One slow walk at dusk

Summer

The days are long, and most of the work is watering and picking. Water in the evening, harvest in the morning, and sit in the shade at midday.

Sow

  • Salad every three weeks
  • Late carrots and beetroot
  • Kale for the winter

Notice

  • Swifts screaming overhead
  • Lime trees humming with bees
  • Warm stone after sunset

Rest

  • Let the lawn grow a little
  • Save seed from the best plants
  • Eat outside, even once

Autumn

Growth slows and the colours turn. It's the time to gather and store, plant bulbs for spring, and leave the seed heads standing for the birds.

Sow

  • Garlic and overwintering onions
  • Tulip and crocus bulbs
  • Green manure on bare beds

Notice

  • Spider webs heavy with dew
  • Geese moving in long lines
  • The smell of wet leaves

Rest

  • Leave a leaf pile for hedgehogs
  • Clean and oil the tools
  • Light a candle at five

Winter

Very little grows, and that's fine. Plan next year, look at the bare shapes of the trees, and sit by a window while the light is short and low.

Sow

  • Nothing outside — plan instead
  • Microgreens on the sill
  • Order seeds by candlelight

Notice

  • Robins singing through the cold
  • Hazel catkins in February
  • Frost ferns on the glass

Rest

  • Let the beds lie under mulch
  • Mend one thing slowly
  • Go to bed with the dark

The moon

One cycle, twenty-nine and a half days

Old gardeners sowed leafy crops on the waxing moon and roots on the waning. The science is thin, but it's a good excuse to look up every night.

Field guide

Six plants worth knowing by heart

Scroll sideways. Each one is common, forgiving and generous, and good company for a first garden.

  • Rumex acetosa

    Sorrel

    A lemony perennial that comes back every spring before almost anything else. Pick the young leaves.

    PerennialPart shade

  • Calendula officinalis

    Pot marigold

    Bright, edible petals from June to frost. It seeds itself around the garden, politely.

    AnnualFull sun

  • Salvia officinalis

    Common sage

    Soft grey leaves that smell of warm stone. It thrives on neglect and poor soil.

    ShrubFull sun

  • Lunaria annua

    Honesty

    Purple spring flowers, then papery silver seed discs that catch the low winter light.

    BiennialAny light

  • Beta vulgaris

    Rainbow chard

    Crimson and gold stems that shine through a winter frost. Cut leaves as you need them.

    BiennialSun or shade

  • Helleborus niger

    Hellebore

    The Christmas rose flowers in the depths of winter, nodding quietly toward the soil.

    PerennialShade
